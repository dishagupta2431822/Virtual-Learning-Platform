Tutorial & Theory

Polarization & Malus's Law

Polarization is a property of transverse waves specifying the geometrical orientation of the oscillations. In light waves, the electric field vector oscillates in a specific plane.

Malus's Law

When completely polarized light is incident on an analyzer, the transmitted intensity $I$ varies directly as the square of the cosine of the angle $\theta$ between their transmission axes: $I = I_0 \cos^2\theta$.

Crossed Polarizers

When two polarizers have their transmission axes perpendicular ($\theta = 90^\circ$), complete extinction occurs ($I = 0$).

Experiment Objectives

  • Verify Malus's Law across varying analyzer angles ($\theta$).
  • Observe zero light transmission at crossed angles ($90^\circ$).
  • Analyze cosine-squared intensity distribution profiles.
