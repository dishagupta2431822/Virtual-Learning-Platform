Tutorial & Theory

Standing Waves on Strings

When a string fixed at both ends is driven at specific resonant frequencies, incident and reflected transverse waves interfere to produce standing waves characterized by stationary nodes and antinodes.

Wave Speed ($v$)

The speed of transverse waves along a stretched string depends directly on tension $T$ and linear mass density $\mu$: $v = \sqrt{T/\mu}$.

Resonant Frequencies ($f_n$)

Harmonics occur at discrete frequencies where string length $L$ equals an integer multiple of half-wavelengths: $f_n = \frac{n}{2L}\sqrt{\frac{T}{\mu}}$.

Experiment Objectives

  • Observe fundamental and overtone standing wave patterns ($n = 1$ to $5$).
  • Analyze wave speed variations with tension ($T$) and mass density ($\mu$).
  • Count node and antinode positions across harmonic loops.
