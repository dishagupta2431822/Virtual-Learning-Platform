Tutorial & Theory

Quantum Theory of Light

The photoelectric effect is the emission of electrons when electromagnetic radiation (light) hits a metal surface. Albert Einstein explained it using Planck's quantum hypothesis.

Einstein's Equation

$K_{\text{max}} = h\nu - \Phi$. The maximum kinetic energy of emitted electrons depends solely on photon frequency $\nu$, not light intensity.

Work Function ($\Phi$)

The minimum energy required to liberate an electron from a metal surface. Below threshold frequency $\nu_0 = \Phi / h$, no electrons are emitted regardless of intensity.

Experiment Objectives

  • Determine Planck's constant ($h \approx 6.626 \times 10^{-34}\text{ J}\cdot\text{s}$).
  • Verify threshold frequency ($\nu_0$) and stopping potential ($V_s$) relationship.
  • Observe photocurrent independence from photon frequency above threshold.
